# Now Playing detail sheets — issue 206

This local v0.6 candidate starts from `371cec970593bc464ff03d1042e2c94b91ff999a`.
It changes presentation and optional detail composition, not player, transport,
request admission, or audio-session ownership.

## Observed baseline

Two native synthetic UI regressions failed before the runtime edits on the retained
iOS 27.0 simulator. A Light-root app presented the Now Playing album sheet with a
Dark detail environment. Artist → album navigation from the sheet changed the
detail context from related to ordinary browsing. Retained screenshots also show
ordinary browsing chrome and a More Like This section in that pushed sheet.

The source had two presentation-wide Dark preferences and explicitly reset the
related-sheet environment on every collection destination. These are concrete
appearance and navigation-context defects. The synthetic baseline did not establish
a cause for every reported freeze, nor demonstrate that SwiftUI zoom transitions
are inherently broken in sheets.

## Change

The player's artwork and transport retain their local Dark environment. On iOS,
the full player also retains its presentation-level Dark preference for status-bar
contrast. Its presenter captures the live system scheme outside that boundary and
passes it independently to the related sheet's own presentation preference.
Related detail backgrounds and action chrome use semantic system styling.
Full-player artwork treatment stays separate; macOS remains unforced at the
presentation level.

Pushed collection destinations inherit their existing related-sheet context and
use ordinary native navigation there. Root browsing retains its artwork zoom
transition. The related presentation also retains local navigation on macOS instead
of escaping through the main window's navigation coordinator.

The first candidate exposed a second concrete interaction defect. After scrolling
the artist sheet, dragging its displayed handle moved/overscrolled the content
instead of dismissing the sheet. The recording retained the large sheet through
the strict five-second dismissal deadline. The sheet hid the native indicator and
drew a decorative, touch-through capsule over its scroll view. Restoring the native
drag indicator reserves native gesture ownership for this scrolling presentation.
The full player's separate fading hint remains unchanged. No general grabber or
gesture recognizer rewrite was needed.

Now Playing album recommendations and artist Appears On/More Like This sections
are omitted where those independent section consumers would otherwise be created.
Their optional requests therefore do not start. Required detail, track and artist
discography work remains available; ordinary browsing retains optional sections,
caching and independent publication. No shared request scheduler changes.

## Verification boundary

The DEBUG synthetic harness exposes the actual detail environment and counts only
explicit fixture requests. Native tests cover Light/Dark, medium/large detents,
reopening, artist → album → back navigation, playback identity, absence of optional
sections and unchanged optional request counts while normal recommendations remain
available. Required catalog/track requests may advance independently.

## Native results

All runs used the retained iOS 27.0 simulator and synthetic fixtures. Logs,
xcresults, screenshots and the failure recording remain in local milestone
validation evidence; raw artifacts are not published.

- Baseline: the two strict context/Light assertions failed as described above.
- First candidate: three native cases passed: three artist → album → back cycles
  with retained context/playback identity; Light and Dark album sheets at both
  detents across two openings each. Independent screenshot review confirms semantic
  row backgrounds/control contrast while the artwork hero remains legible.
- First candidate recommendation case failed its existing dismissal helper after
  scrolling the artist sheet. This was a runtime gesture conflict, not an excuse
  to weaken the assertion or extend its deadline. Initial menu actions in the
  navigation case also logged two native 60-second animation-idle warnings before
  continuing; their cause is not established and smooth interaction is not claimed.
- After the native-indicator-only correction, the unchanged targeted recommendation
  case passed. Both sheets dismissed after three content scrolls; Pause returned,
  playback identity stayed equal, and the optional counters stayed at album 1,
  artist 0, appearances 0. Normal album recommendations remained available.
  The three previously passed cases were not blindly repeated after this narrow
  handle change; combined integration verification remains distinct.
- Final source: macOS Debug passed 66 focused tests; iOS UITesting passed 67.
  Both runs select `FoundationPresentationTests` and `FoundationLibraryActionsTests`,
  including destination ownership, cancellation/late-result rejection, related
  metadata, independent publication and retained-page checks. Both platform
  branches compile. Formatting and whitespace checks pass.

Verification deviation: the first focused iOS unit run mistakenly used Debug
instead of UITesting and completed before it could be stopped. It passed 67 tests
but installed/launched the canonical simulator test host. The scheme passes
`-foundationTesting`; the DEBUG `restore()` guard returns before normal cache and
credential restoration. This is not a blanket claim of zero data access. No
normal-session launch, sign-in, cleanup, uninstall, rollback or account-data
inspection was performed. The built and installed canonical bundle identifiers
were verified; no prior inventory establishes preservation of an earlier binary.
The exact scope was then rerun successfully with UITesting and its installed
`.uitesting` bundle identity verified. This corrects subsequent verification, not
the historical deviation. Physical apps were not installed or changed.

## Presentation-owner follow-up

Expanded review of the original Light screenshots found a contrast regression
missed by the initial row/control review: the first patch changed the status bar
over dark purple artwork from white to black. A content-local Dark environment
does not preserve native presentation chrome. This was a new regression and is
not described as an accepted baseline behavior.

The follow-up restores `preferredColorScheme(.dark)` only on the iOS full player.
The existing presenter captures its live `colorScheme` in a dedicated optional
environment value, in both native full-screen and existing custom-host paths.
The related sheet applies that captured value to its own preferred scheme, so
the parent's Dark preference cannot mask system changes. No process-global
window queries or additional UIKit bridge are introduced. macOS retains its
existing content-local treatment and unforced related presentation.

The new opt-in native test runs without a fixture appearance override. It first
opens the album sheet in real system Light Mode, signals a readiness marker, then
checks the open medium sheet after the serialized host changes the simulator to
Dark. At the large detent it signals another marker and checks a real change back
to Light. It dismisses and reopens the sheet, checking playback controls and the
retained Light scheme. The host restores the original simulator appearance in a
`finally` block and records the restoration result.

The test requires runner environment `VELACANTO_SHEET206_LIVE_APPEARANCE=1`;
ordinary UI runs skip it. The authorized host passes this using the verified
`TEST_RUNNER_` prefix, then responds to `VELACANTO_SHEET206_READY_DARK` and
`VELACANTO_SHEET206_READY_LIGHT` with `simctl ui <retained-device> appearance`.
Its new 30-second orchestration waits do not change existing five-second detail,
detent or dismissal assertions. The real-system test executed and passed in
71.992 seconds, with both markers observed and original Light appearance restored.
It was not skipped. Existing Dark and Light reopening cases also passed unchanged
in 71.625 and 67.597 seconds. Final presentation tests passed 26 on macOS Debug
and 26 on iOS UITesting; both platform branches compile.

Independent visual review of the new Light and Dark captures confirms white
status-bar icons over the artwork and correctly themed sheet rows and action
controls. This is scoped synthetic evidence, not a claim that every artwork palette
or device configuration has been checked.

Physical responsiveness, native-handle visual acceptance, physical system
appearance changes, VoiceOver and physical playback acceptance remain separate
checks. No physical installation, publication or broad unresponsiveness root-cause
claim follows from synthetic tests.
